# Thomas in the East: the Gospel of Thomas, the Gondophares strand, and the limits of the Chinese record

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.24409357.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24409357)

Author: Atharva Chourey

## Abstract

This paper asks three dated questions about Thomas in the East: when was the Gospel of Thomas composed, does the Chinese record date anything about Thomas himself, and what does the dated evidence establish about a Thomas mission to India? The Gospel of Thomas is dated to c. 70–140 CE for the collection substantially as we have it, over a sayings core plausibly reaching c. 50–100, forced from above by the Greek papyri — P.Oxy 1 (shortly after 200), P.Oxy 654 and P.Oxy 655 (3rd century), and P.Oxy 5575 (published 2023), in which Thomas material is already interwoven with Matthew and Luke by the late 2nd century — and by Hippolytus (c. 222–235). The Chinese record gives NO BITE on Thomas himself: the Xi'an Stele (781) never names him, and the Zunjing catalogue's 35 titles contain no Thomas text, a verified absence across the surviving Jingjiao, Chinese and Turfan Manichaean, and Bulayïq remains, while the tradition's carrier, the Church of the East, is firmly attested in China (Alopen 635, edict 638, renaming 745, stele 781). For India, the Northwest strand is graded PLAUSIBLE–UNPROVEN: the Acts of Thomas (Syriac, c. 200–250) names King Gudnaphar and his brother Gad, and Gondophares (r. c. 20–46) appears in no Greek or Roman literary source, the Acts remaining the only literary witness to him until 1854. The South Indian community is ATTESTED from c. 550, with a Thomas tomb-cult in India by c. 590 and the tomb fixed at Mylapore by c. 1298, but the Malabar itinerary in its received form is first written in the 16th century. The community anchors are FIRM — a Persian-linked church by c. 550 and a chartered church at Kollam in 849/850 — and silent on the founder.

*Keywords:* Gospel of Thomas; Acts of Thomas; Gondophares; Jingjiao; Xi'an Stele; Zunjing; Malabar; Mylapore; Church of the East

## Key results

- The Gospel of Thomas is dated to c. 70–140 CE for the collection substantially as we have it, over a sayings core plausibly reaching c. 50–100 — forced from above by P.Oxy 1 (shortly after 200), P.Oxy 654/655 (3rd century), P.Oxy 5575 (published 2023; Thomas material interwoven with Matthew/Luke by the late 2nd century) and Hippolytus (c. 222–235).
- China gives NO BITE on Thomas himself: the Xi'an Stele (781) never names him and the Zunjing catalogue's 35 titles contain no Thomas text — a verified absence across the Jingjiao, Chinese/Turfan Manichaean and Bulayïq remains; the Psalms of Thomas are a different work and never travelled east in any identified manuscript.
- The tradition's carrier is firmly attested in China: Alopen's arrival in 635, Taizong's edict in 638, the Daqin renaming in 745, and the Xi'an Stele in 781.
- The Northwest strand is PLAUSIBLE–UNPROVEN, the strongest strand: the Acts of Thomas (Syriac, c. 200–250) names King Gudnaphar and his brother Gad, and Gondophares (r. c. 20–46; Takht-i-Bahi inscription → 46 CE on the Azes=Vikrama era identification, flagged) appears in no Greek or Roman literary source — until 1854 the Acts was the only literary witness to him.
- The strongest counter-evidence is the silence: Origen's allotment gives Thomas Parthia and India to Bartholomew, and the Malabar form — 52 CE landing, seven churches, 72 CE martyrdom — is first written in the 16th century.
- The South community is ATTESTED from c. 550 (Cosmas), with a Thomas tomb-cult in India by c. 590 and the tomb at Mylapore by c. 1298; the community anchors are FIRM — a Persian-linked church by c. 550 and a chartered Kollam church in 849/850 — and silent on the founder.
